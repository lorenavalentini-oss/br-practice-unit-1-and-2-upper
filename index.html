Speaking practice · Real business situations

Speak for 1–2 minutes per task, or work with a partner. Use the target vocabulary, not just general English.

Professional image

Describe how a prospective client might form an opinion of your company before a meeting. Use at least five expressions: reputation, project an image, come across, trustworthy, build a relationship, online profile.

Website consultant

You are advising a company with an ineffective website. Compare a functional, trustworthy design with an ostentatious, complex one. Explain three improvements.

Arrange a meeting

Role-play: You received a referral, call a prospective client, ask whether they have reviewed your portfolio, propose a provisional meeting and discuss transport.

Contact details

Role-play: Ask for a colleague’s email and phone number, give your business card and promise to send another contact by text.

Present tenses

Describe your permanent responsibilities, regular routines, current projects, temporary duties and next week’s arrangements.

Speaking practice · Real business situations

Speak for 1–2 minutes per task, or work with a partner. Use the target vocabulary, not just general English.

What motivates you?

Rank salary, autonomy, fulfilment, recognition, development and job security. Explain which three matter most and why.

Design a benefits package

You are launching a company. Propose four material benefits and four non-material benefits, using exact vocabulary from the unit.

Internship debate

Would you accept an unpaid internship? Discuss hands-on experience, menial tasks, career ladder, references and long-term benefits.

Conference small talk

Role-play: Meet a colleague, break the ice, ask open questions, react with Really? / What a coincidence! / That sounds interesting, then end politely.

Question forms

Ask a colleague eight questions, including three indirect questions, two negative questions and three question tags.
